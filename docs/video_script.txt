ArcTreasury Copilot is an AI-powered treasury operations platform that automates USDC payouts across multiple blockchains using Circle's developer tools and Arc Testnet. Let me walk you through the entire system.

Starting with the Dashboard. This is your command center. At a glance, you can see total batches, total payout volume, status distribution, and recent activity. The stats update in real time with animated number counters. You can also toggle between Mock, Circle, and Arc adapter modes right from the header, which controls whether payouts are simulated or executed on real chains.

Moving to the Batches page. This is where payout operations begin. You can create batches manually, or click Seed Demo to generate sample payout items across multiple recipients and chains. Once a batch is created, you run the Policy Engine, which evaluates every item through five rules: amount limits, chain validation, recipient risk scoring, velocity checks, and duplicate detection. Each item gets a risk score from zero to one hundred, mapping to one of five decisions. Approved, Queued, Review, Held, or Rejected. But the real highlight is the AutoPilot button. One click and it runs the entire pipeline automatically: seed data, run policy, auto-approve review items, and execute payouts. The whole process completes in about ten seconds.

Next is the Execution Monitor. Here you can track every payout in real time. Each item shows its execution status, the adapter used, and whether it was a real or simulated transaction. Failed items can be retried with a single click. You can drill into any item to see its payout legs, which track each step of the transfer including source chain, destination chain, transaction hash, and attempt number.

The Copilot Agent page is where automation gets serious. This is an autonomous background agent that continuously runs a loop: create batches, run policy, auto-review, and execute. You choose from three strategies. Conservative only auto-approves low-risk queued items. Balanced handles queued and review items under a risk threshold of fifty. Aggressive auto-approves nearly everything. You can start, stop, and switch strategies in real time, and the activity log shows every action the agent has taken.

Now the Treasury page, which has three tabs. The first tab shows RWA positions. Idle USDC is allocated to four tokenized asset classes: US Treasury Bills at five point two five percent, a Money Market Fund at four point eight percent, Corporate Bonds at six point one percent, and a Real Estate Fund at seven point five percent. You can see a pie chart showing portfolio diversification, a reserve ratio bar with health status, and category breakdowns by AUM. There's also an allocation panel where you select an asset and invest, with minimum investment validation built in.

The second tab is Circle Gateway, the fiat on and off ramp. You can select from three payment methods: Wire Transfer, ACH, or SEPA. Each shows supported currencies, minimum and maximum amounts, settlement time, and fees. When you select Wire Transfer and choose Euro, a live FX conversion preview shows exactly how many USDC you'll receive. All gateway transactions are logged in a table below with the FX rate applied.

The third tab is the CCTP Bridge, powered by Circle's Cross-Chain Transfer Protocol V2. It supports seven chains: Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum, Solana, Avalanche, Base, and Arc, giving forty-two directional bridge routes. You pick source and destination chains, enter an amount, and the system plans the optimal route showing burn, attest, and mint steps with fees and estimated time.

The Audit Log page captures every state transition in the system. Batch creations, policy decisions, manual reviews, agent actions, execution results, treasury allocations, gateway transactions. Everything is timestamped, filterable, and provides full before and after state visibility for compliance.

Finally, the Settings page lets you configure the system. Toggle between Mock, Circle, and Arc adapters. Adjust policy thresholds like maximum payout amount and velocity limits. View wallet configuration and supported chains.

And this is not just a prototype. We completed four real USDC transfers on Arc Testnet using Circle Programmable Wallets. Real wallet, real transactions, real on-chain hashes. Every simulated element is clearly labeled so you always know what's real.

ArcTreasury Copilot shows that on-chain infrastructure can power real-world financial operations today. Automated, compliant, and yield-generating. Thank you.
